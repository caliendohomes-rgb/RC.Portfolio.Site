About

From strategy to execution system

I work where executive strategy meets Product and Engineering reality. At Kaseya, I lead programs across the Cyber Resilience and Backup SaaS portfolio: consolidating a multi-product Backup portfolio onto a natively built object-storage data plane, governing execution of the Unified Cyber Resilience Portal, and leading AI adoption and enablement for 1,500+ engineers and product leaders.

My pattern is consistent. Leadership defines the destination; ownership, sequencing, and dependencies are not yet aligned. I build the structure that is missing: workstreams, roadmap standards, live dashboards tied to source data, escalation paths, and a clear line between what delivery teams can commit to and what depends on decisions made elsewhere.

Before technology, I spent more than a decade in organizational change, leadership development, and behavioral health. That background is why adoption, incentives, and cross-team friction go into the plan from day one instead of surfacing after launch.

FLAGSHIP · Portfolio Transformation In progress

Backup Portfolio Consolidation and Object-Storage Migration

The challenge.

Executive strategy called for consolidating a multi-product Backup portfolio and moving toward a more unified Cyber Resilience architecture. Teams did not yet share migration definitions, workstream ownership, sequencing, or roadmap maturity, and some Product and Engineering leads were unclear how their work mapped to the broader migration.

The strategy.

Standardize on a natively built object-storage data plane as a shared foundation for: lower infrastructure cost, product consolidation onto fewer strategic platforms, public-cloud deployment, FedRAMP and regulated-market readiness, and future unified Cyber Resilience workloads.

Lower infrastructure cost Strategic platforms Public-cloud deployment FedRAMP readiness Unified workloads

My role.

I lead the program mechanics that turn this strategy into predictable migration execution: migration workstreams, Jira-aligned roadmaps with epic hygiene standards for Product Owners, a live portfolio dashboard tied to source epics, per-product migration sessions, office hours, and escalation paths for unresolved cross-functional decisions.

The hard part.

Migration sequencing depends on more than Engineering delivery. It requires coordination across Product, Architecture, Infrastructure, Security, Finance, cloud-provider strategy, customer migration, and regulatory requirements. I make those dependencies visible and distinguish executable engineering commitments from milestones that remain contingent on upstream architectural, commercial, or leadership decisions.

Consolidate portfolio Migrate products and customers Standardize on object-storage data plane Lower infrastructure cost Enable public-cloud deployment Foundation for FedRAMP and regulated markets

AI Transformation

Enterprise AI Adoption at Scale

The problem.

AI coding tools were arriving fast, but adoption across a 1,500+ engineer organization was inconsistent, ungoverned, and impossible to tie to business value. Leadership needed enablement that actually moved delivery velocity, not a pile of unused licenses.

The action.

Led AI adoption and enablement for 1,500+ engineers and product leaders: consolidating tooling across Claude, Codex, Cursor, and Lovable (with Claude as the primary adoption focus), and building the governance, adoption tracking, workflow integration, and a peer-led AI Enablement community with best practices, responsible-use principles, onboarding support, and MCP showcases and rules of use.

The outcome.

A measurable, dashboard-tracked productivity lift and executive ROI models linking AI investment to productivity and cost efficiency, informing multi-million-dollar funding decisions.

Strategy-to-Execution Readiness

Diagnosing a Stalled Growth Objective

A company growth objective centered on a cloud backup offering was not converting into meaningful new revenue. I investigated why and found the gaps were not in engineering: seller awareness, CRM data quality, incentive alignment, and GTM activation across Product, Marketing, RevOps, Sales, and Compensation. The core finding: the objective had been treated as an execution commitment before the commercial operating system required to deliver it was validated.
